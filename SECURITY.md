# Security Policy

Thank you for helping keep our projects, software, and users safe. We welcome good-faith security reports and treat them with care, urgency, and respect.

This policy applies to all repositories in the `@dnbhq` organisation.

If you believe you have found a security issue, report it privately and do not open a public issue, discussion, pull request, or social media thread about it.

## Reporting a Security Issue

Email security reports to [security@davids-neighbour.com](mailto:security@davids-neighbour.com).

Include as much detail as you can:

- The affected repository, package, service, or URL.
- A clear description of the issue and its impact.
- Steps to reproduce, proof-of-concept code, screenshots, logs, or requests where useful.
- Any conditions that make exploitation possible or impossible.
- Whether you have shared the report with anyone else.

We will acknowledge your report within 48 hours. We will then review the issue, ask follow-up questions if needed, and share the next steps as soon as we can.

You must not access, change, delete, exfiltrate, or publish data that does not belong to you. You must not degrade, disrupt, or deny service to our systems or users. If you accidentally encounter sensitive data, stop immediately and include only the minimum information needed for us to verify the issue.

## Disclosure Policy

Security issues must remain private until we have investigated, fixed, and coordinated disclosure. Public disclosure before coordination is not acceptable.

When we receive a security report, we will assign a primary handler to coordinate the response. The handler will:

- Confirm the issue and determine the affected repositories, packages, services, and versions.
- Assess impact, severity, and exploitability.
- Audit for related issues where appropriate.
- Prepare and release fixes as quickly as practicable.
- Credit the reporter when appropriate and when the reporter wants credit.
- Coordinate any public advisory, release note, or announcement.

We reserve the right to decline reports that are abusive, speculative, non-actionable, or outside the scope of this policy.

## Spam, Solicitation, and AI-Generated Reports

We have a strict no-spam and no-solicitation policy. Fake reports, bulk submissions, marketing outreach, list-building, lead generation, and unrelated messages sent through this reporting process or to its email address are violations of this policy.

AI-assisted reports are allowed, but the reporter is fully responsible for their contents. Before submitting, verify that the report is accurate, actionable, and relevant to a real security issue in an affected `@dnbhq` repository.

## Out of Scope

Report vulnerabilities in third-party dependencies, services, or platforms to the maintainers of those projects unless the issue is caused by our use or configuration of them.

The following are usually out of scope unless they demonstrate a concrete security impact:

- Missing security headers without an exploitable path.
- Automated scanner output without verification.
- Social engineering, phishing, or physical attacks.
- Denial-of-service testing without prior written approval.
- Issues requiring access to an already-compromised account, device, token, or local environment.

## Questions and Improvements

If you have suggestions for improving this policy, open a public issue or pull request only for policy wording. Do not include vulnerability details in public channels.
